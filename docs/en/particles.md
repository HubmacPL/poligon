# Particles

Rain, snow, dust in the light, an animal's breath, flies, falling leaves. Two halves: the game moves particles (`poligon/world/particles.h`, in `poligon_world`), the renderer only draws them (`Renderer::DrawParticles`, `poligon/render/particles.h`). So particles are part of the game's state, repeatable and dumpable, and the renderer stays free of simulation.

No library was taken: SPARK (the best-known C++ particle engine) is LGPL, which the license rules forbid, and Effekseer (MIT) is a whole effects editor with its own runtime and renderers for Direct3D and Vulkan, not SDL GPU, made for effects authored in its editor rather than state the game owns and dumps. What is needed here, an emitter with a box, velocities, a lifetime and colors over life, is about 200 lines.

## Simulation: ParticleEmitter

- `ParticleEmitter(const ParticleEmitterSettings&, std::uint64_t seed)`; `SetPosition` (the middle of the box new particles start in; a rain box follows the player), `SetSettings` (live particles keep how they started), `SetEmitting`, `Update(float delta_seconds)` in the game's `FixedUpdate`, `Burst(count)`, `GetParticles()` (a `std::vector<Particle>` ready for drawing), `GetParticleCount()`.
- `ParticleEmitterSettings`: `rate` per second (the fraction carries over to the next tick), `max_particles`, `lifetime` and `lifetime_variation`, `box_size`, `velocity` and `velocity_variation`, `acceleration` (gravity, wind), `drag` (share of the velocity lost per second, applied as `exp(-drag * dt)`), size and color from start to end of life (linear colors; alpha fades them in and out), `rotation_speed` and its variation, `atlas_frames` with `frames_per_second` (0: a random picture each).
- Repeatable: the random numbers come from the emitter's own SplitMix64 generator (one 64-bit word; `std::mt19937` has 2.5 KB of state and the C++ library's distributions differ between implementations), and the dead are removed keeping the order of the rest, so the same seed and the same calls give the same particles to the bit (same machine and executable, as everywhere in the engine).
- State: `DumpStateToJson()` writes the generator, the carried fraction, the position and every particle with its age, with 9 significant digits like the ECS state dump; `HashState()` is its FNV-1a hash. The ECS dump does not take vectors, so a game that keeps an emitter in a component dumps or hashes it through these.
- Test: `tests/world/particles_test.cpp` (two runs of 180 ticks with a burst and a move give the same dump, another seed another; one particle falls, grows and dies as its settings say). Example: `examples/world/particles` (rain and dust).

## Drawing: Renderer::DrawParticles

- `void DrawParticles(TextureHandle texture, const Particle* particles, int count, const ParticleOptions& options)`. `Particle`: `position`, `size` (meters), `velocity` (only for stretching), `rotation` (radians, in the card's plane), `color` (linear, alpha for transparency), `frame` (picture of the atlas). `ParticleOptions`: `blend` (`Alpha` or `Additive`), `lighting` (`Simple`: the sun with its shadows and the clouds', plus sky light, without normals; `None`: own colors), `atlas_columns`, `atlas_rows`, `stretch` (cards stretched along their velocity on screen to `size + speed * stretch`: rain), `soft_distance` (meters over which a card fades where it nears the scene behind it).
- Each call is one instanced draw of cards facing the camera (64 bytes a particle, 6 vertices made in the vertex shader; `FacingAxes` in `common.hlsli`, shared with impostors), sorted from back to front on the processor within the call; calls are drawn in order. Meant for some thousands of particles a frame.
- Place in the frame (`passes/particle_pass.cpp`, shaders in `shaders/particles/`): after water, clouds, volumetric fog and temporal anti-aliasing, before bloom, exposure and tone mapping. After TAA, because cards that move without motion vectors would smear in its history (rain would become fog); before bloom and exposure, so that they glow and adapt like the scene. The depth image hides cards behind the scene (each fragment compares its own distance with the scene's) and softens them where they near it (`SoftFade`, shared with the water's shore). Fog and the haze of distance are applied at each particle's own distance (`ApplyDistanceFog`, shared with lit surfaces); the volumetric fog between the camera and a particle is not added, because that fog is computed for the scene's depth only: particles far into thick volumetric fog look too clear. With particles the depth pass always runs.
- Test: `tests/render/particle_render_test.cpp` (a card colors its pixels; behind a wall it is hidden; 0.1 m in front of the wall with a soft distance of 0.3 m a third of it shows, 156 against 155 expected; lit by a sun of 0.5 it is half white, 187 against 188; two additive cards of 0.25 add to 0.5).
- Limits: no collisions with the ground (rain falls through it, hidden by the depth test; a game can kill particles below the ground itself), no lights of their own, no shadows cast by particles, no sorting between calls (draw far groups first), cards only (no meshes or ribbons).
