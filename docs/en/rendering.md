# Rendering

Headers: `poligon/render/renderer.h`, `poligon/render/graphics_quality.h`, `poligon/world/sky_occlusion.h`, `poligon/core/math.h`, `poligon/core/image.h`, `poligon/core/platform.h`.
Examples: `examples/render/renderer`, `examples/render/texture`, `examples/render/environment`, `examples/assets/model`, `examples/render/temporal_aa`, `examples/render/graphics_quality`, `examples/world/water`, `examples/render/clouds`. Shaders: `engine/src/render/shaders/<group>/*.hlsl`.

## How it works

- `Renderer` draws every frame to an off-screen image (1280x720 by default). With a window that image is then copied to the window; without one it can only be saved. The pixels are the same in both modes.
- Order within a frame: `BeginFrame(camera, light)`, any number of `DrawMesh(mesh, transform, material)` (or with a plain color), `EndFrame()`. After `EndFrame` you may call `SaveFrameToPng(path)` or `CaptureFrame(image)`.
- Meshes and textures live until the renderer is destroyed; create them outside `BeginFrame`/`EndFrame`.
- The background color (`RendererConfig::clear_color`) is set once when the renderer is created.
- Unrecoverable problems (no graphics device, calls in the wrong order) end the process through `FatalError`.
- Engine headers do not expose SDL types.

## Frame stages

A frame is recorded by `DrawMesh` calls and carried out in `EndFrame`, in these passes (those turned off are skipped):

1. **Shadows.** The scene's depth as seen from the sun, four times (**cascades**): the visible range up to `Environment::shadow_distance` is cut into four parts, near ones sharp, far ones coarse (the splits are computed from 1 m out, so the sharpest cascade is not spent on the first step in front of the camera). All four share one depth image as 2 x 2 tiles. Each cascade is a sphere around its part of the view, moved in whole shadow pixels only, so shadows do not crawl when the camera turns or moves. A lookup reads a tent filter 5 shadow pixels wide (9 bilinear comparisons) with the point moved off the surface by the filter's reach along the normal, which gives soft edges without steps or self-shadowing. **Shadows that soften with distance from their caster** (percentage-closer soft shadows, Fernando 2005): with `Environment::sun_angular_size` above 0 (degrees; the real sun is 0.53, `forest_scene` uses it) and `soft_shadows` on, 9 reads 3 shadow pixels apart find the average depth of what blocks the sun around the point, the distance to it and the sun's size give the penumbra, and the tent filter is stretched to match, from its own width up to twice it. No random sampling, so the picture stays still from frame to frame. Settings file: `shadows.sun_size`, `quality.soft_shadows`. Cost: below the noise of measurement (0.3 ms or less on the GTX 980). Test: the soft shadow part of `tests/render/effects_render_test.cpp` (the edge of a plate's shadow from 10 m up is wider with a sun of 3 degrees than with the fixed filter, and the same in two frames). Limits: the penumbra is capped at twice the filter (the blocker search reaches only that far), so in the sharpest cascade, where the filter is a few centimeters, a crown's shadow is only a little softer than a trunk's; the widening is a stretched tent, not a true disc. Over the last tenth of its range each cascade fades into the next, and the last one into full light, so no line shows where sharpness changes or where shadows end. Terrain skirts (the rims that hide gaps between patches) are left out of the shadow pass: seen from the sun they are walls, and on a slope they would shadow the land below them in a grid.
2. **Cloud shadows** (with `Environment::clouds` and `cloud_shadows`). Over 8 km around the camera, a 256 x 256 image of the share of sunlight the clouds let through (`cloud_shadow.frag.hlsl`, 16 steps towards the sun through the cloud layer). Every lit surface and the volumetric fog multiply the sun by it, so cloud shadows drift over the land and the light shafts in the fog go out under them. The steps read the clouds' detailed shape, the one the sky shows (the base shape alone, along a path slanting kilometers through the layer, met some cloud almost everywhere and left the whole land in shade), and light scattered forward inside a cloud still gets through, so thin clouds cast grey shadows rather than black (three scattering orders, Wrenninge 2013, as in Hillaire's Frostbite clouds). `post.debug_view cloud_shadow` shows the image from above (north up, the camera in the middle).
3. **Depth and ambient occlusion** (`Environment::ambient_occlusion`). The scene's depth as seen from the camera is drawn first, with one sample per pixel. From it Intel's **XeGTAO** (ground-truth ambient occlusion, Jimenez et al. 2016, as Intel wrote it; MIT, github.com/GameTechDev/XeGTAO, its files `XeGTAO.h` and `XeGTAO.hlsli` taken as they are into `shaders/third_party/xegtao` with the license) works out, per pixel, how much of the sky's light nearby geometry hides (crevices, the inside of a tree crown, where a trunk meets the ground); the scene pass gives such places less ambient light, direct sunlight is not affected. It runs as SDL GPU compute pipelines (`renderer/gtao.cpp`, entry points `shaders/post/gtao_*.comp.hlsl` modelled on Intel's `vaGTAO.hlsl`): view-space depth with four smaller levels, the occlusion term with normals worked out from depth and its edges, one edge-aware blur, then `gtao_apply.frag.hlsl` puts the result into the ambient occlusion image with `ambient_occlusion_strength` (0 to 1; 1 = as XeGTAO gives it). Its constants come from its own `GTAOUpdateConstants`, with the depth unpacking our reversed depth needs; `ambient_occlusion_radius` is its radius in meters; the preset's `ambient_occlusion_samples` chooses Intel's quality level (up to 8 low: 1 slice of 2 steps, 12 medium: 2 of 2, 20 high: 3 of 3, more ultra: 9 of 3; the loops are unrolled, so each level is a shader of its own). Full float precision, no wave operations, nothing the GTX 980 lacks. Changes for SDL GPU, in our files only: the occlusion term and the result are unsigned integer images, which SDL GPU does not let be sampled, so they are read as storage images, and the blur (Intel's `XeGTAO_Denoise`, copied into `gtao_denoise.comp.hlsl`) reads the term with four `Load`s in place of `GatherRed`. With temporal anti-aliasing the noise moves every frame (`NoiseIndex`) and is averaged away. Cost against our former estimate, 800 frames at 1920 x 1080 on the GTX 980: medium 0.2 to 0.4 ms less, high the same (12.4 to 13.6 ms) in the four measured views. Close-ups of a trunk's foot, a rock and an oak crown before (our own estimate) and after are in `poligon-assets/previews/gtao`. Limits: only what is on screen, only the nearest surface of each pixel (a thin leaf counts as a solid block behind it); no bent normals yet (XeGTAO can give them, for aiming the sky light). Test: the ambient occlusion parts of `tests/render/effects_render_test.cpp`: a 0.3 m cylinder on a floor seen from 2 m up, ambient light only: 0.44 on the floor 1 cm from it, 0.50 at 2 cm, 1.00 one meter away (limits 0.25, 0.3 to 0.7, above 0.95), a lit floor at its foot half as bright as open floor (above 20%); 1 cm from a tall wall 0.30, where the cosine-weighted share of sky is 0.5: XeGTAO's final power of 2.2 is Intel's fit to its ray-traced reference (limits 0.22 to 0.4).
4. **Scene.** Sky first, then meshes, in linear colors with a wide range (values above 1 are kept), with multisampling (`RendererConfig::msaa_samples`, 4 by default). Cut-out materials use **alpha to coverage**, so leaf edges are smooth.
5. **Water** (`Renderer::DrawWater(mesh, transform, WaterMaterial)`; lakes of generated land get their meshes from `CreateLakeSurfaces` in `poligon/world/water.h`). Drawn after the scene, over its single-sample image, testing and writing the depth image, so that fog and TAA treat the surface like any other. It reads copies of the scene's color and depth as they were without it (`water.frag.hlsl`): small waves tilt the surface (three sizes of moving noise, drifting with the wind; `wave_size`, `wave_strength`); what lies under the water is read a little off to the side where waves bend the view (`refraction`; not where that would land on something above the water) and dimmed per color by the path through the water (`absorption`, red first), while the water's own color, lit by sky and sun, takes over with depth (`color`, `scattering`); a reflected ray is walked through the depth image (**screen-space reflections**, `Environment::water_reflection_steps` up to `water_reflection_distance` meters) and mirrors what it meets on screen, the sky elsewhere; Fresnel's law mixes the two, the sun adds a highlight shadowed like everything else, and where the water is only centimeters deep the surface fades into the ground under it (`shore_fade`). Limits: only what is on screen can be mirrored (the far shore's trees yes, what is behind the camera no); no planar reflections; the camera under water is not handled; streams are not water surfaces. Test: `tests/world/water_render_test.cpp`; example: `examples/world/water`.
6. **Clouds** (`Environment::clouds`, only with `draw_sky`). Where the scene shows the sky, the line of sight is walked through a layer of air (`cloud_bottom` to `cloud_top`) in a smaller image (`RendererConfig::cloud_resolution`, a quarter of the drawn size by default), after Schneider (Horizon Zero Dawn, 2015) and Hillaire (Frostbite, 2016): a weather map says where clouds stand and of which type (`cloud_coverage` moves a threshold on the shape, so it is not the share of sky covered; `cloud_variation` says how much the map varies it across the land, from the same everywhere at 0 to clear and overcast areas kilometers across at 1), a 64 x 64 x 64 Perlin-Worley volume gives their shape (`cloud_size` meters per repeat) and a 32 x 32 x 32 Worley volume erodes their edges; light at each step is sunlight through the cloud above (a few steps towards the sun), shaped by a two-lobed phase function (a silver lining towards the sun) and the "powder" darkening of thin edges, plus sky light stronger near the top; each step is integrated energy-conserving. The image is built up over frames: each frame walks one pixel of every 2 x 2 block, in turn, and `clouds_resolve.frag.hlsl` takes the rest from the previous frame's image at the place the line of sight showed there (only the camera's turn matters for things kilometers away); a walked pixel takes a tenth of its new value, so about ten walks are averaged; each 2 x 2 block starts its steps at a random offset (a hash of the block, moved on by the golden ratio with every walk, the four pixels a quarter step apart), while the steps towards the sun stay in the middle of their stretches (moved with each walk, they made sunlit parts shimmer). (A gradient noise over pixels, read at every other pixel, showed as a regular grid of dots that shimmered.) After a camera jump, on the first frame or after a frame without clouds every pixel is walked. So the clouds cost about a quarter of what walking every pixel did. Limits: at a cloud image of the full drawn size (no preset uses it) faint horizontal bands remain, from the few steps across the sharp bottom and top of the layer; steps that shorten inside clouds would remove them. Far towards the horizon the clouds fade into the haze. They drift downwind at `cloud_speed` with `wind_time`, so replays match. The noise volumes are made in code (about a second; `Renderer::PrepareClouds` does it while loading). Clouds also make the sky's light greyer, by up to half at full coverage. Cost on the GTX 980 in `forest_scene` at high: about 1 ms. Test: the cloud part of `tests/render/effects_render_test.cpp`; example: `examples/render/clouds`. Limits: a flat layer (no Earth curvature), no light inside clouds from lightning or the ground, the camera always below the layer.
7. **Volumetric fog** (`Environment::volumetric_fog`, uses `fog_density` and `fog_color`). At half resolution, every line of sight is walked in 32 steps; each step adds the light the fog scatters there and dims what lies behind. Steps in the sun's shadow add less (`fog_shadow_brightness`), which shows as **shafts of light** between trees. The fog glows around the sun (`fog_sun_glow`), can thin out with height (`fog_height_falloff`, `fog_base_height`) and also covers the sky. It replaces the simple distance fog of the scene pass.
8. **Blended materials** (`Material::blend = BlendMode::Blend`: glass, windows). Over the scene with its fog, farthest object first; see [Blended transparency](#blended-transparency).
9. **Temporal anti-aliasing** (`PostSettings::temporal_aa`, off by default). Every frame the camera is shifted by a different fraction of a pixel (8 points of the Halton sequence) and the new frame is blended into the previous result (`temporal_aa_blend`, 0.1: about the last ten frames count). For each pixel the motion since the previous frame says where the point it shows was, and the previous result is read there with a Catmull-Rom filter. The motion comes from **motion vectors**: with TAA on, the depth pass also writes each pixel's motion on screen (`depth_motion.frag.hlsl` into a two-channel half-float image), from the previous frame's unshifted camera, the previous wind (so plants in the wind are followed), the previous transform of a mesh drawn with an `object_id` (`DrawMesh`, `DrawSkinnedMesh`, `DrawModel`; any nonzero number that stays the same for the object, several draws with one id told apart by their order) and, for skinned meshes, the previous pose. Where nothing wrote a motion (sky, impostors) the depth image and the two cameras give it. The nearest surface in the 3 x 3 neighborhood decides the motion, so that edges of near objects move with them. What the motion misses (shading changes, places just uncovered) is handled by clipping the history into the range of colors the new frame's neighborhood holds (mean plus or minus 1.25 standard deviations in YCoCg, with colors squeezed by brightness first), which removes ghosts. Faster-moving pixels take more of the new frame. With TAA on, the noise patterns of ambient occlusion, fog steps and dithered fades change every frame and are averaged away. The depth pass runs whenever TAA is on. MSAA is not needed with it (`forest_scene` turns it off and saves 3 ms); `sharpen` gives back some of the softness. A camera jump of more than 25 m, or `Renderer::ResetTemporalHistory()`, starts fresh. `forest_scene` gives ids to its crates and foxes. `post.debug_view motion` shows the motion image. Limits: a moving mesh drawn without an id counts as still (only the clipping catches it); the trample map's change is not followed. Tests: `tests/render/temporal_aa_test.cpp`, the motion vector part of `tests/render/effects_render_test.cpp` (a box moved with an id has motion, without one none, a plant in the wind has motion) and of `tests/animation/skinning_render_test.cpp` (the swung half of a bar has motion from its previous pose); example: `examples/render/temporal_aa`.
10. **Bloom** (`RendererConfig::bloom`, `bloom_strength`). The scene is shrunk into a chain of ever smaller, blurrier copies, which are then mixed back together and blended into the picture: bright areas get a soft glow.
11. **Tone mapping and grading.** The wide-range image is turned into displayable colors: `exposure` scales brightness, **automatic exposure** (`RendererConfig::auto_exposure`) first brings the log average of the picture's brightness (the mean of logarithms over 64 x 64 points of the half-size image, so that the sun or a bright patch of sky does not outweigh the rest; in two steps, a 16 x 16 image of means over 4 x 4 points, then their mean, because one pixel reading all 4096 points took about half a millisecond) to `auto_exposure_target`, adapting over game time, not frames: the brightness the eye is adapted to moves towards the picture's by `1 - exp(-t / T)` over a frame of `Environment::frame_seconds` = t, with the time constant T = `auto_exposure_brighter_seconds` (0.5 s) when the picture got brighter and `auto_exposure_darker_seconds` (2 s) when it got darker, as an eye adapts faster to light than to dark (all the way on the first frame); since `exp(-a) exp(-b) = exp(-(a + b))`, the same time split into any number of frames gives the same exposure (games pass `FrameInfo::delta_seconds`, the game time the frame's ticks covered, so replays match; the default is 1/60), `ToneMapping::Aces` (the default) rolls bright areas off smoothly, `ToneMapping::AgX` keeps the hue of very bright colors as they bleach towards white (a sunset or a fire stays orange where ACES turns it yellow; "AgX Minimal" by Benjamin Wrensch, MIT, a polynomial fitted to Troy Sobotka's AgX, in `shaders/post/agx.hlsli` with its notice), `ToneMapping::AgXPunchy` adds its "Punchy" look (more contrast and saturation), `ToneMapping::None` clips (exact; used by tests). Settings file: `post.tone_mapping none|aces|agx|agx_punchy`. Test: `tests/render/tone_mapping_test.cpp` (an orange 4 times as bright as white shows hue 36 degrees under AgX against 52 under ACES; mid gray stays gray, 128; Punchy darker in the shadows, 42 against 70). Grading (`PostSettings`): `temperature` and `tint` (white balance, divided by its own brightness so it does not change exposure) and `saturation` act in linear light before the curve, `contrast` acts around middle gray in a square-root scale after it, then the `vignette`; `sharpen` works on the anti-aliased image before exposure, limited to the range of the four neighbors so that edges do not ring.

**Repeatable frames with temporal effects.** TAA (and later clouds) use previous frames, yet a headless run must give the same picture every time. This holds because everything a frame depends on is a function of the frames before it, all of which are rendered the same way: the sub-pixel shift and the noise offset come from the renderer's own frame counter (never the clock), the history is two images written in turn inside the renderer, and a new renderer starts with no history. So the same program, settings and sequence of frames give the same pixels to the bit on one machine and backend (checked by `temporal_aa_test`, which renders the same 17 frames twice and compares the bytes). Tests that check single pixels keep TAA off (the default), so their first frame is not blended with anything; `forest_scene --headless --ticks N` renders a frame on every tick, so a dump after N ticks is always the N-th frame of the same sequence.

**Wind** moves plants in every pass (so shadows move too). `Environment::wind_direction`, `wind_strength` (1 = a moderate breeze, 0 = off) and `wind_time` (the game's own time in seconds, so that replays match) describe the frame's wind; `Material::wind_bend` and `wind_flutter` say how much a mesh gives to it. In the vertex shaders (`ApplyWind` in `common.hlsli`) the whole plant leans downwind by `wind_bend` x strength x about 3 cm per meter above its foot (the model's origin), with gusts that travel across the land as two waves and start at a slightly different moment for every plant, and each vertex shivers on its own by `wind_flutter` x strength x about 4 cm. Trees take bend 1 and flutter 1 on their leaves; short plants take a larger bend (grass 6), because they bend more per meter. Example: `examples/world/wind`; test: the wind part of `tests/render/effects_render_test.cpp`.

Before the passes, meshes are **culled**: a mesh whose bounding box lies entirely outside the camera's view is not drawn, and each shadow cascade skips meshes that cannot cast a shadow into it (a mesh outside the view still can). `Renderer::GetFrameStats()` returns how many meshes were submitted and drawn in the last frame.

Many copies of one mesh are drawn with `DrawMeshInstances` (one draw call per pass, each copy culled on its own); forests go through the vegetation system built on it. See [vegetation.md](vegetation.md).

## Graphics quality

Header: `poligon/render/graphics_quality.h`. Example: `examples/render/graphics_quality`.

- The scene decides how the picture looks (fog color, shadow range, how far plants are drawn); `GraphicsQuality` decides how much it may cost. Four ready presets: `low`, `medium`, `high`, `ultra`. `GetGraphicsPreset(preset)` gives a preset's values; any single value can then be changed.
- A preset is picked in three ways, all with the same result: `quality.preset medium` in the render settings file (applied first, wherever it stands; values the file names after or before it then win), `--quality medium` on the command line (a game passes `CommandLineOptions::quality` to `ParseGraphicsPreset`), or in the settings window, where picking a preset sets all its values.
- Values that need a new renderer (shadow image size, multisampling, drawing resolution) go into the `RendererConfig` through `ApplyGraphicsQuality(quality, config)`. Switches and step counts limit every frame through `ApplyGraphicsQuality(quality, environment, post)`: a switch only turns an effect off that the scene asked for, the shadow range is multiplied. The scales for plants, ground cover and terrain are for the game to pass to its systems (`forest_scene` does it in `ApplyQualityToSystems`).
- **Render scale** (`render_scale`, only with TAA): the scene, depth, ambient occlusion and fog are drawn at that share of the output's width and height; TAA, which runs at the output size, fills the detail back in from the frames' sub-pixel shifts (each output pixel weighs the drawn samples by their distance from its center). Bloom and tone mapping run at the output size. With TAA off the drawing size is always the output size.
- **Far cascades** (`far_shadow_scale`, `RendererConfig::far_shadow_scale`): the two far shadow cascades use only that share of their tile's width and height (the top-left part). They cover large areas where fine shadow detail is lost anyway, and in a forest the cost of a shadow image grows with its pixel count, because tree crowns overlap many times as seen from the sun.
- Two things in the renderer make all presets cheaper. Without multisampling the depth pass always runs and the scene pass then tests that depth before shading ("equal or nearer", no depth writes, `[earlydepthstencil]` in `mesh_after_depth.frag.hlsl` and `impostor_after_depth.frag.hlsl`), so each pixel is lit once instead of once for every leaf behind it. And instances are drawn nearest first: from the camera in the view, from the sun in each cascade, so the depth test rejects most hidden leaves before their pixels are shaded (ties keep the instances' order, so the result is the same on every run).

## Sky from the atmosphere and the time of day

`Environment::sky_model = SkyModel::Atmosphere` draws the sky from a model of the air instead of the three hand-given colors (`SkyModel::Simple`, still the default). The model and its numbers are Hillaire's ("A Scalable and Production Ready Sky and Atmosphere Rendering Technique", 2020), ported from his code (github.com/sebh/UnrealEngineSkyAtmosphere, MIT, Copyright (c) 2020 Epic Games; the license text is at the top of `shaders/common/atmosphere.hlsli`): an Earth of 6360 km with air up to 100 km, Rayleigh scattering (blue sky, red sunsets) thinning out over 8 km, Mie scattering (haze, the glow around the sun) over 1.2 km, ozone around 25 km.

- **The sun's place:** `ComputeSunDirection(time_of_day, day_of_year, latitude_degrees)` in `poligon/render/sky.h` gives the direction sunlight travels (as `DirectionalLight::direction`; +x east, -z north). It uses the usual approximation of the sun's declination (within about half a degree) and solar time, without the equation of time. With `Environment::time_of_day` at 0 or above the renderer puts the sun there itself (`day_of_year` 172, `latitude` 50.86: Batorz, by default); below 0 it keeps `DirectionalLight::direction`.
- **The sun's light and the sky's colors:** `ComputeSkyLight(sun_direction)` gives, per unit of sunlight above the air, the share that reaches the ground (redder and weaker as the sun sinks, 0 below the horizon, fading over the sun's own width) and the sky's colors straight up, at the horizon and from the ground. With the model on, `DirectionalLight::color` means the sun above the air: the renderer multiplies it by that share and replaces `sky_zenith`, `sky_horizon` and `sky_ground` with the model's, so the sky light map, clouds, fog, the haze of distance and the ambient light all follow the time of day. The units: a white matte surface facing the sun shows `color * share`, one under an even sky of radiance L shows `pi * L * color`.
- **How:** on the processor (`render/sky/sky.cpp`, once, on first use) a 256 x 64 transmittance table (how much light gets from a point to the top of the air, by height and angle; 40 steps) and a 32 x 32 table of light scattered twice or more (64 directions around each point, the orders of scattering summed as a geometric series, ground of albedo 0.3); both go to the graphics card as half floats. Every frame `passes/atmosphere_pass.cpp` builds a 192 x 108 sky view table (light reaching the viewer from every direction, rows denser near the horizon, columns by the angle from the sun's bearing; 4 to 14 steps each with single scattering from the transmittance table plus the multiple scattering table). The sky (`sky.frag.hlsl`) and the sky light map (`sky_capture.frag.hlsl`) read it; the sun's disc is the sun's light through the air in front of it. The sky is seen from 200 m above sea level, wherever the camera is.
- Settings file: `sky.model simple|atmosphere`, `sky.time_of_day` (hours, -1 = off), `sky.day_of_year`, `sky.latitude`. `forest_scene` uses the model with the sun at its old direction and a white sun of 3.6 above the air; a settings file with `sky.time_of_day` moves the sun to that hour. Pictures of the four measured views at 5:00, 8:00, 12:00, 18:00 and 20:30 (high) are in `poligon-assets/previews/day`.
- The tables take about 0.1 s on the first frame with the model; the model's light is computed again only when the sun moves.
- Test: `tests/render/sky_render_test.cpp` (the sun at noon on 21 June at 50.86 N stands 62.6 degrees up in the south, rises in the east, has set at 21:00; a low sun's light is redder and weaker and the horizon redder; on screen the sky towards a sun 5 degrees up has a red-to-blue ratio 1.87 against 0.35 for one 60 degrees up; the zenith gets brighter at each of five sun heights, 2 to 70 degrees; both backends).
- Limits: no moon, stars or night sky (after the blue hour the sky goes black; left out to stay within the estimate); the haze of distance is still the simple exponential one, colored by the model's horizon (not Hillaire's aerial perspective volume); clouds are lit by the model's sun and sky colors but do not shade the sky view table; the viewer's height does not change the sky (no flights above the clouds); solar time, not clock time.

## Light from the sky, leaves and distance

- **The sky light map** (with `sky_light`): the sky with its clouds is captured into a small cube map around the camera (64 pixels per face, `sky_capture.frag.hlsl`, the clouds walked with fixed steps, the sun's disc left out) and blurred level by level as ever rougher surfaces reflect it (`sky_prefilter.frag.hlsl`, GGX importance sampling after Karis 2013, each sample read from a level of the captured sky that matches its share of directions); the last level, 4 x 4, is the cosine-weighted light a matte surface receives. Every lit surface takes its ambient light from that level in the direction it faces, so shade has the color of the real sky before it, clouds included, and its reflections from the level for its roughness: the direction bent towards the normal as roughness grows, nothing from below the surface's horizon, the split-sum BRDF in closed form (Karis 2014 after Lazarov) with the energy rough surfaces would otherwise lose, and less reflection where ambient occlusion (Lagarde and de Rousiers 2014) or the sky occlusion map hide the sky. Water mirrors it where its screen-space reflection finds nothing, so clouds show in lakes. The map is renewed over 8 frames (one face a frame, then the smaller levels, then the blurred levels, which change all at once), or all at once on the first frame. Cost: about 0.5 ms on the GTX 980. Without `sky_light`, ambient light is two colors: `ambient` of the sun from above and below. Test: the sky light map part of `tests/render/effects_render_test.cpp` (a floor facing up takes the sky's color, a ceiling the ground's, smooth metal mirrors the sky). Limits: the map is seen from the camera, so a valley floor far away reflects the sky as the camera sees it; a little of the other half's light reaches a surface (up to about 8%), because the ambient level is so small.
- **Large-scale occlusion:** `Renderer::SetSkyOcclusion(&map)` with a `SkyOcclusionMap` (`poligon/world/sky_occlusion.h`): how much of the sky each place on the land sees, from the horizon's height in eight directions out to 160 m (`MakeTerrainSkyOcclusion`) and from tree crowns above it (`AddCanopy`). Sky light on every surface is multiplied by it (ground light by 0.6 to 1 of it), so valley floors, gullies and the forest floor under crowns are darker than open ground, which screen-space ambient occlusion cannot see. `forest_scene` makes it for 2.4 km around the start at 4 m, with about 25000 crowns, in 0.4 s. Limit: a crown gets the value of the ground under it.
- **Light through leaves:** thin foliage (double-sided materials) lit from behind glows in a warmer, more saturated green (light passes the leaf's color twice), most when the camera looks towards the sun through it; `Environment::foliage_translucency` sets how much (0.35 by default, 0.5 in `forest_scene`).
- **Haze of distance (aerial perspective):** `Environment::aerial_density` (per meter; 0 = off). Every lit surface and water fade with distance into a haze of the sky's horizon color, bright and warm towards the sun. With `fog_sky_color` (0 to 1, with `sky_light`) the haze, simple fog and volumetric fog take, by that share, the color of the blurred sky light map just above the horizon in the direction one looks (in the direction itself for volumetric fog), clouds included, instead of the horizon's and `fog_color`; `forest_scene` uses 1. Far hills turn blue without any fog near the camera. `forest_scene` uses 0.00012 (half the light is haze at about 6 km).
- Test: the sky occlusion and haze parts of `tests/render/effects_render_test.cpp`.

## Materials and light

- `Material` is the metallic-roughness model of glTF: base color (times `texture`), `metallic`, `roughness`, optional `normal_texture` (fine relief) and `orm_texture` (green roughness, blue metallic, red ambient occlusion when `occlusion_in_orm`). Data textures are created with `TextureOptions::is_color = false`.
- **Emission** (lamps, embers, screens): `Material::emissive` (a linear color, black = none) times `emissive_strength`, times `emissive_texture` (a color texture) when there is one, is light the surface gives off itself, in the units of other light (a white matte surface facing a sun of color 1 is as bright as emissive 1). It is added after lighting and before fog and haze, so it shows in the dark, fades into fog with distance, and feeds bloom and automatic exposure like any bright pixel. Models bring it from the file: glTF `emissiveFactor`, `KHR_materials_emissive_strength` and `emissiveTexture` (Assimp's emissive color, intensity and emissive texture for other formats), kept through cooking. Limit: it lights nothing around it (no light of its own; a lamp that should light the ground needs a light source, which the renderer does not have yet). Test: the emission part of `tests/render/effects_render_test.cpp` (a floor in total darkness is black, with emissive 1 at strength 0.5 exactly 0.5, an emissive texture colors it, a small bright one feeds bloom), and `tests/assets/model_test.cpp`, `tests/assets/assets_test.cpp` for loading and cooking; example: `examples/render/emissive`.
- Normal maps follow the OpenGL convention (green points to the top of the image). Meshes need no tangents; the shader derives them per pixel.
- `DirectionalLight` is the sun: `direction` it shines in, `color` (may exceed 1), and flat `ambient` light. Units: a white matte surface facing the sun reflects exactly the sun's color.
- `Environment` (third argument of `BeginFrame`): `draw_sky` (gradient from `sky_horizon` to `sky_zenith`, `sky_ground` below, sun disc), `sky_light` (surfaces are lit by the sky from above and the ground from below instead of flat ambient; smooth surfaces mirror the sky), `shadows` and `shadow_distance`, `fog_density` and `fog_color`. The default is plain: no sky, no fog, shadows on.
- Double-sided materials are treated as thin foliage: light from behind shines through a little.
- A sunny scene has light values of 2 to 4: turn on `auto_exposure`, or use an exposure around 0.5 to 0.7.
- The new effects are off by default, so that a plain scene stays plain and exact; `examples/render/environment` turns all of them on.
- `tests/render/scene_render_test.cpp` checks shadows, sky, fog, normal map orientation, multisampling and tone mapping by pixel values on both backends; `tests/render/effects_render_test.cpp` does the same for ambient occlusion, volumetric fog, bloom and automatic exposure.
## Conventions

- Math is GLM, used directly (`glm::vec3`, `glm::mat4`, `glm::quat`, `glm::translate`, `glm::lookAt`...). Always through `#include "poligon/core/math.h"`, never through GLM headers, and without your own `GLM_FORCE_*`: the `poligon_engine` target sets the configuration.
- Right-handed world, +Y is up, a camera looks down its local -Z axis.
- 4x4 column-major matrices, column vectors: `a * b` applies `b` first. The usual object transform is `translate * rotate * scale`.
- Angles for GLM functions are in radians: `glm::radians(degrees)`.
- Normals are turned into the world with the inverse transpose of a transform's 3 x 3 part, so they stay square to the surface under any scale, also a non-uniform scale after a rotation (as under a stretched parent). For `DrawMesh` and `DrawSkinnedMesh` it is computed on the processor and passed with the object's other values (one more matrix per draw). Instances (`DrawMeshInstances`) use the cofactor matrix instead, worked out in the vertex shader from the instance's own matrix (three cross products): the inverse transpose times the determinant, the same direction after normalizing, with no second matrix stored per instance (that would add 48 to the 64 bytes of each of the hundreds of thousands of plant instances). Its sign follows the determinant, so a mirrored instance (negative scale) gets reversed normals; mirroring also flips triangle winding and is not supported. Test: the normals part of `tests/render/effects_render_test.cpp` (a cube turned 45 degrees and stretched 3 times along y, lit from straight above: its slanted face gets n.y = 0.316 of the sun, not the 0.949 the model matrix would give).
- Clip-space depth runs from 0 to 1 (`GLM_FORCE_DEPTH_ZERO_TO_ONE`). The camera uses it **reversed**: 1 at the near plane, 0 at the far one (near and far swapped in `glm::perspective`, depth cleared to 0, depth test "greater"). With a floating-point depth image this keeps millimeters of precision kilometers away, where the usual direction has meters and distant land, trees and impostors fight over pixels. Shadow cascades keep the usual direction. Shaders that read the camera's depth treat 0 as the sky.
- A default-constructed vector is zero, a matrix identity, a quaternion no rotation.
- Vectors have only `x`, `y`, `z`, `w` members (no aliases such as `.r` or `.s`).
- `tests/core/math_test.cpp` guards these conventions.
- Triangle front faces are counter-clockwise. Indices are 32-bit.
- Texture coordinates (UV): (0, 0) is the top-left corner of the image, (1, 1) the bottom-right. glTF uses the same.
- Material and light colors are linear. The output image and color textures are stored sRGB-encoded (like image files and displays), and the graphics card converts both ways. The background color is given as it should look on screen.

## World positions and camera-relative drawing

A `float` has 24 bits of precision: 16 km from the origin it holds positions only to about 1 mm, and what is drawn there shakes and splits. So, as in Unreal, the engine keeps world positions in `double` and lets the graphics card see only positions relative to the camera.

- **Double:** world positions only, as `glm::dvec3` (and `glm::dvec2` for x, z on maps): object placement, physics bodies and characters, the camera, ECS components that hold where something is, the player's start, particle emitters, decals, the origins of terrain, water, vegetation cells and ground cover cells.
- **`poligon::Transform`** (`poligon/core/math.h`) is a world placement: `position` (dvec3), `rotation` (quat), `scale` (vec3). It replaces a `mat4` with a world translation wherever one was passed: `DrawMesh(mesh, transform, material)`, `DrawSkinnedMesh`, `DrawDecal`, `DrawModel`, `PhysicsWorld::GetTransform`, `Vegetation::AddInstance`.
- **Origin plus local matrix:** a `mat4` stays for everything local (a part of a model, a joint, a tilt). Calls that need a full matrix take a double origin and a float matrix relative to it: `DrawMesh(mesh, origin, local, material)`, `DrawMeshInstances(mesh, material, origin, transforms, count)`, `DrawImpostorInstances`, `DrawWater`, `DrawParticles(texture, origin, particles, count)`. Instances are stored relative to their cell's corner, particles relative to their emitter's origin (`ParticleEmitter::GetOrigin`). `RelativeTo(origin, local, new_origin)` moves such a matrix to another origin.
- **Float:** everything else: sizes, directions, velocities, colors, local matrices, distances.
- **The renderer has no double inside.** `BeginFrame` takes the camera with its double position; every `Draw...` subtracts the camera from its position in double on the processor and stores a float position relative to the camera. The camera is at 0 in every matrix the card sees (`view_projection` holds no camera position). World maps (terrain heights and weights, trample map, sky occlusion, cloud shadows) get their corner relative to the camera; vegetation cells and terrain patches work out their origin relative to the camera once per frame.
- **Things fixed to the world** keep their place with a shift worked out in double: texture tiling of the terrain (the phase of each repeat at the camera, so textures do not slide), the mottling of steep ground (relative to the terrain's corner), wind gusts and water waves (relative to the nearest whole multiple of 4096 m: `NoiseShift` in `renderer_internal.h`, so the noise repeats every 4096 m, which no one sees).
- **Clouds and the sky capture** work in absolute float coordinates (`FrameContext::camera_world`, `world_view_projection`): clouds are kilometers in size, and a few centimeters of error there are invisible.
- **Motion vectors** for temporal anti-aliasing: the previous frame's `view_projection` is relative to the previous camera, so it is moved by the camera's step (`previous_view_projection = previous * translate(current - previous)`); the previous transform of each object is moved the same way.
- **Shadow cascades** snap to their texel grid in double, in the light's turned coordinates, so shadow edges do not swim when the camera moves far from the origin.
- Tests: `tests/world/far_from_origin_test.cpp` draws one scene (terrain, meshes, plants, water, a decal, particles, a skinned mesh) at the origin and moved by (20480, 0, 20480) (five squares of the 4096 m grid that wind and waves repeat on), with the wind blowing and waves on the water, on both backends, and `image_compare` requires the two pictures to match with the tolerance used between backends (measured: largest channel difference 16 of 255, 2 pixels of 57 600 over 12).
- All water of a frame anchors its waves to the camera's square of the grid (since 2026-10-26; before, each surface to its own origin), so that pieces of one lake drawn as separate surfaces, as a streamed world's patches are, meet without a seam.
- The moment each plant starts the gusts (its phase) comes from smooth noise over the land (`WindNoise` in `common.hlsli`, two turns over a few meters) since 2026-10-26. Before, it was a hash of the plant's position (`frac(sin(dot(...)) * 43758.5)`), which jumps at the smallest change of rounding: moving the vegetation's cells to fit streamed patches gave whole trees another phase, and `forest_scene`'s crowns swayed differently in 2.6% of the pixels of a view. `game/tests/toy_world_test.cpp` and `tests/physics/physics_test.cpp` do the same for the simulation (see [physics.md](physics.md)).

## Textures and materials

- `Image` (`poligon/core/image.h`): RGBA bytes, rows from the top. `LoadImageFromFile` reads PNG and JPEG, `SaveImageToPng` writes, `MakeCheckerImage` and `MakeSolidImage` create images in code.
- `Renderer::CreateTexture(image, options)` copies an image to the graphics card. Default: a color image (sRGB) with mipmaps. `is_color = false` for images that hold data other than color.
- **Mipmaps** are smaller copies of a texture used when it is drawn small or far away; without them distant textures shimmer.
- `Material` has a color, a texture and `uv_scale`. The color multiplies the texture; a material without a texture has the plain color; `uv_scale` repeats the texture.
- `alpha_cutoff` above 0 makes pixels with lower alpha invisible (**alpha cutout**): the way leaves are drawn, as flat squares with a picture of a twig. `double_sided` draws both sides of every triangle and lights the back as if its normal were flipped. Models get both from the file (glTF alpha mode and `doubleSided`). `back_faces_keep_normal` lights the back with the front's normal instead (grass blades, whose normals point up). `casts_ambient_occlusion = false` leaves a material out of the depth that ambient occlusion is computed from; it is added to the depth image right after, so the scene pass, water, fog and TAA still see it (grass: the screen-space method takes a thin blade for a solid wall). `normal_up` (0 to 1) turns the normal that far towards straight up before lighting, on both sides: grass at 1 is lit like the flat ground it stands on, whichever way its cards face. None of the three is in model files; code sets them (for plants through `PlantSettings`, see vegetation.md).
- There is one sampler for all textures: linear filtering with mipmaps, anisotropy 8, repeat outside the 0..1 range.
- Mipmaps are computed on the CPU (the same on every backend). For the color texture of a cut-out material (`TextureOptions::coverage_cutoff`, set by `UploadModel` from the material's cutoff) every mipmap has its alpha scaled so that the share of pixels above the cutoff stays as in the full image. Without this, averaging a twig picture down pushes its alpha below the cutoff, and crowns thin out with distance and vanish from the coarse shadow cascades, leaving only the shadows of trunks and branches.
- `tests/render/texture_render_test.cpp` renders headless and checks single pixels (orientation, colors, repetition, mipmaps) on both backends. It is the pattern for rendering tests without reference images.

## Models

Headers: `poligon/assets/model.h` (model data, uploading, drawing), `poligon/assets/model_import.h` (`LoadModelFromFile`; tool side). Example: `examples/assets/model`. Tool: `model_viewer`.

- `LoadModelFromFile(path, model, error)` reads a model through the Assimp library: glTF/GLB, FBX, OBJ, DAE and about forty other formats. It needs no graphics card.
- The loaded `Model` follows our conventions whatever the file used: right-handed, +Y up, counter-clockwise front faces, UV (0, 0) at the top-left.
- A model file is a tree of nodes with transforms. Loading flattens it into **instances**: "draw this mesh with this transform". `Model::bounds_min/max` is the box around everything.
- Each material has a base color and, if present, a decoded base color texture (from a file next to the model or embedded in it). A texture that cannot be loaded gives an entry in `Model::warnings`, not an error.
- `UploadModel(renderer, model)` copies meshes and textures to the card; `DrawModel(renderer, render_model, transform)` draws them.
- `model_viewer` started with a double click browses the models in `poligon-assets/source` (N and P switch to the next and previous one); `model_viewer FOLDER` browses a folder; `model_viewer FILE` shows one model. Camera: left mouse drag, A/D, W/S, Q/E. `model_viewer FILE --headless --dump-frame out.png` writes one picture and prints triangle counts, size and warnings; this is how the agent looks at a model.
- Too heavy a model (film-quality trees have millions of triangles): `tools/assets/simplify_model.py` runs in Blender without its window and writes a glTF with a given part of the triangles kept: `blender -b --factory-startup -P tools/assets/simplify_model.py -- INPUT OUTPUT.gltf 0.1`. Blender is an external tool; nothing from it is linked.
- Impostors (far-away cards with baked pictures of a model) and the dithered fade between levels of detail are described in [vegetation.md](vegetation.md).
- Generated trees: `tools/assets/make_tree.mjs` runs the EZ-Tree library (MIT) under Node.js and writes glTF files with levels of detail: `node tools/assets/make_tree.mjs NODE_MODULES ASSETS_FOLDER RECIPE.json`. A recipe (ours are in `tools/assets/trees/`) gives an EZ-Tree preset with changes, the height, bark and twig textures (color, normal map, occlusion-roughness-metallic), the rectangles of a twig atlas (a texture holding several twigs; each card shows one, at its own proportions) and the levels. The full tree goes to `generated/NAME/`, the simpler levels to `lods/NAME/lodN/`, the layout `forest_scene` reads. Simpler levels come from the same tree: fewer sides on each tube, thin branches left out, fewer but larger cards; the script stops with an error if a level would change the tree's shape. The format is described at the top of the script. EZ-Tree and three.js are installed outside the repository (`poligon-assets/tools/eztree`); ASSETS_FOLDER is `poligon-assets/source`.
- `model_viewer FILE TURN TILT DISTANCE` sets the starting view, for close-up pictures.
- `forest_scene` (in `tools/`) builds a piece of forest from the models and ground textures in `poligon-assets` and lets you walk through it (W/S/A/D, Shift to run, drag the mouse to look). It is the showcase for the renderer and the place to measure frame time: `forest_scene X Z TURN TILT --headless --ticks 60 --dump-frame out.png` prints milliseconds per frame and how many meshes were drawn. `--quality NAME` picks a graphics preset and `--resolution WxH` the picture size; times per preset are in the Graphics quality section. The forest is the same on every run (fixed random seed). `--record FILE` and `--replay FILE` write and play back the input as in `toy` ([input.md](input.md)). Models that are missing are skipped. Over the picture it shows a crosshair and a line of text in the built-in font (position, heading, number of prints; no clock, so that headless frames repeat).
- Downloaded and simplified assets live in `poligon-assets/source/` next to the project (`polyhaven/`, `lods/`, `generated/` and more; see [assets.md](assets.md)), never in the repository.
- Leaves with a solid background around them: some packages (Poly Haven glTF) ship the leaf color as a JPG with no transparency and the mask as a separate file. The loader then warns that a cut-out material's texture has no transparent pixels. Repair: `image_tool merge-alpha COLOR ALPHA OUT.png` joins the two into one PNG (and spreads leaf colors into the transparent pixels, so edges get no dark rim), then `node tools/assets/set_gltf_cutout.mjs MODEL.gltf OLD_IMAGE_URI NEW_IMAGE_URI` points the model at it and marks the material as cut-out and double-sided. The untouched file stays as `MODEL.gltf.original`. `image_tool pack-orm OCCLUSION ROUGHNESS OUT.png` joins separate occlusion and roughness images into the one data texture glTF expects (red occlusion, green roughness, blue metallic = 0).
- Formats that cannot mark a material as a cut-out (FBX, OBJ): if at least 1% of a material's texture is transparent, the material is treated as a double-sided cut-out (fur, whiskers, leaves). glTF files say it themselves and are taken at their word.
- Skeletons and animation clips are loaded; see [animation.md](animation.md).
- Not loaded yet: cameras and lights from the file, animations of rigid parts. Mirrored transforms (negative scale) would flip triangle winding.
- **Levels of detail for single meshes** (animals, buildings, anything drawn with `DrawMesh` or `DrawSkinnedMesh` rather than as plants): a `MeshLodGroup` lists `MeshLod {mesh, distance}` levels, nearest and most detailed first, up to `kMaxMeshLods` (8), like Unity's `LODGroup`. `int Renderer::DrawMesh(const MeshLodGroup&, transform, material, object_id)` and `int DrawSkinnedMesh(const MeshLodGroup&, transform, material, joint_matrices, joint_count, object_id)` draw the level the distance from the camera to the transform's origin calls for and return it (-1 beyond the last distance: nothing drawn). Over the last `fade_width` of each level's range (0.1 by default) the level dissolves into the next with the dithered pattern of plants (`KeptByFade`, carried to the shaders in the object's uniforms), in the picture, the depth pass and the shadows alike, so there is no pop; the two draws share every pixel exactly. Skinned levels are all posed by the same joint matrices. `FrameStats::mesh_lod_draws[level]` counts the draws per level. For cooked models `MakeMeshLodGroup(loaded, instance, distances)` (`poligon/assets/assets.h`) builds the group from the asset list's `lod` files: the full model's mesh, then the mesh of the node with the same name in each simpler level; a skinned simpler level is loaded as a skinned mesh with its joints matched to the full model's skeleton by name. Test: the levels-of-detail part of `tests/render/effects_render_test.cpp` (two levels told apart by color: near the first, far the second, beyond the last nothing, mid-fade both with every pixel covered, plain and skinned) and of `tests/assets/assets_test.cpp` (a cooked skinned model's simpler level); example: `examples/render/mesh_lod`. `Environment::mesh_detail` (set from the preset's `quality.mesh_detail` by `ApplyGraphicsQuality`; separate from `vegetation_detail`, since plants are counted in tens of thousands and these in tens) multiplies every group's distances; tested by drawing one group 8 m away in low (simpler level) and ultra (detailed). Limit: the distance is to the object's origin, not its size on screen.

## Backend

- Default: **Direct3D 12**. Vulkan is built and tested as the second one (`--backend vulkan`).
- Reason: the D3D12 debug layer is part of Windows and works out of the box (`--gpu-validation`). Vulkan validation layers are not installed on this machine (they need the Vulkan SDK), so the same mistake passes silently there.
- In headless mode the renderer turns off Vulkan "implicit layers" (overlays and recorders of other programs, such as OBS or RivaTuner), which otherwise load their code into the process.
- Tests render the same frame on both backends and compare the images. This keeps shaders portable.
- Frames in flight: with a window, waiting for the swapchain keeps the processor at most two frames ahead of the graphics card. Without one nothing waited until 2026-10-26, and when the card was slower than the processor (forest_scene in high: about 12 ms of the card's work against 5 ms of the processor's), unfinished frames and the resources SDL keeps for them piled up: 0.8 to 1.8 MB more memory every frame. EndFrame now submits a headless frame with a fence and waits for the oldest once more than two are in flight (`kMaxFramesInFlight`). `tests/render/frame_memory_test.cpp` draws 1200 frames in the ultra preset at 3840 x 2160 (so that the card is slower even in the Debug build) and requires the process to hold at most 50 MB more after frame 1200 than after frame 300 (before the fix: 0.78 MB per frame on Direct3D 12, 1.77 on Vulkan; after: 0).

## Window and fullscreen

- `Renderer::SetWindowMode(WindowMode::Windowed, width, height)` shows the game in a window of that size; `WindowMode::Fullscreen` switches the display to exactly that resolution and shows only the game (exclusive fullscreen, not a borderless window). It returns false and changes nothing when the display has no such mode. Call it between frames; the next frame is drawn at the new size, because every image that depends on the size is made again (temporal anti-aliasing starts fresh). Without a window only the picture's size changes, which is how tests check it.
- The window can be resized with the mouse and maximized. `BeginFrame` compares the window's size in pixels with the picture's and, when they differ, calls `Renderer::Resize(width, height)`, which makes every size-dependent image again; temporal anti-aliasing and the clouds start their history afresh, the camera's proportions follow the new width and height, and the debug windows take the new size on their own. A minimized window keeps the last size. `Resize` can also be called by the game between frames (without a window it is the only way). Test: the resize part of `tests/render/effects_render_test.cpp` (a scene drawn after `Resize` matches, within 2 of 255, one drawn by a renderer made at that size); example: `examples/render/window_mode`.
- In `forest_scene` F11 switches between a 1280 x 720 window and fullscreen at 1920 x 1080. Example: `examples/render/window_mode`; test: the window part of `tests/render/effects_render_test.cpp`.

## Occlusion culling by depth

`Environment::occlusion_culling` (`quality.occlusion_culling`, on in high and ultra; `culling.occlusion` in a settings file turns it on for a scene) leaves out of the picture instances, meshes and impostors that the scene itself hides: a hierarchical depth test (HZB-like) on the processor, from the depth of an earlier frame.

- On the graphics card (`passes/occlusion_pass.cpp`, `occlusion_depth.frag.hlsl`): after the depth pass, its image is shrunk to 128 x 72 pixels, each the farthest depth of its block, and copied to a buffer the processor can read; three copies go round.
- On the processor (`renderer/occlusion.cpp`): at the start of a frame the newest copy of a frame at least two submissions back is read (never the frame being drawn; every submission's fence is kept, numbered, and the processor waits only if the card is that far behind). Each of its pixels is put back where it was in the world and seen by this frame's camera (reprojection; where several land in one pixel the farthest counts, where none land nothing is known), then each pixel takes the farthest of its 3 x 3 neighbors, which pulls the edges of what hides inwards. A sphere around an instance, a mesh (its box) or an impostor is left out when its nearest depth lies behind that in every pixel its square covers. Terrain is never left out (it is what hides); shadows are not affected. Counted in `FrameStats::occlusion_culled`. After a camera jump of more than 25 m, or in the first two frames, nothing is culled. The depth pass runs whenever it is on; render targets are not culled this way.
- Deterministic: what a frame culls depends only on frames before it, all rendered the same way.
- Without compute shaders, by choice: a compute pass could test every instance on the card and draw indirectly, but SDL GPU's indirect draws would mean moving every batch to them; the processor's test costs about 0.1 ms for some ten thousand instances.
- Gain on the GTX 980 at 1920 x 1080 (800 frames, with against without): high 0.2 to 0.3 ms faster in the four measured views and two views with a ridge in front; medium 0 to 0.1 ms slower (fewer things far away to hide, and the shrinking and copying cost about as much as it saves), hence on from high only. In the `forest` view it leaves out 3436 of 12428 instances in view.
- Pictures: the same views with and without, 1280 x 720 high, differ in 0 to 30 pixels (thin plants at the edge of what hides; `image_compare` passes them with the usual tolerance).
- Test: `tests/render/occlusion_render_test.cpp` (behind a wall a mesh and five instanced boxes are culled from the third frame on, 6 a frame, a box beside the wall and one in front are not, nothing in the first two frames, nothing with it off, and the picture is the same as without culling to the last channel; both backends).

## Point and spot lights

`Renderer::DrawLight(const PointLight&)` and `DrawLight(const SpotLight&)` (between `BeginFrame` and `EndFrame`; `renderer.h`): a lamp, a campfire, a flashlight. `PointLight`: `position` (double), `color`, `intensity` (a white matte surface facing the light from 1 m away shows color times intensity, as under a sun of that color), `range` (no light beyond it). `SpotLight` adds `direction`, `inner_angle_degrees` and `outer_angle_degrees` (full light inside the inner, none beyond the outer) and `shadows`.

- Light falls with the square of the distance and fades smoothly to nothing at the range (Karis's window, "Real Shading in Unreal Engine 4", 2013). It lights everything `ShadeSurface` lights (meshes, instanced plants, terrain, impostors, decals, water, blended materials) with the same GGX response as the sun, particles with `ParticleLighting::Simple` (all that arrives, whatever the facing), and the volumetric fog.
- At most `kMaxLights` (64) light a frame: those whose reach touches the view, nearest to the camera first; the rest are left out and counted in `FrameStats::lights_skipped` (`lights_drawn` counts the others).
- **Tiles on the processor** (`resources/lights.cpp`): the screen is cut into 48 x 27 tiles whatever its size; each light's sphere is projected to a rectangle of tiles, and each tile keeps a 64-bit mask of the lights that may reach it. A pixel visits only its tile's lights. The lights and masks (14 KB) go to the shaders in a storage buffer (`light_data` in `shaders/common/lights.hlsli`), the rest in uniform buffer 3: SDL GPU's Vulkan backend shows a shader only the first 4 KB of a uniform buffer. Not a compute shader: with 64 lights the processor marks a few thousand tiles a frame, and the shaders need no new kind of resource.
- **Spot light shadows** (`SpotLight::shadows`, `passes/spot_shadow_pass.cpp`): the first four such lights by distance get a shadow image each, 1024 x 1024 in one 2048 x 2048 depth image, drawn like a shadow cascade but through a perspective camera covering the cone; meshes and instanced meshes cast, terrain and impostors do not. Read with 3 x 3 comparisons, the point moved off the surface along its normal by about a shadow pixel. The image is made on the first frame that needs it.
- **In the fog:** the four lights nearest the camera light the volumetric fog, evenly scattered, without shadows, once per line of sight rather than per step: the light's reach cuts a stretch out of the line, over which its falling off is integrated exactly (a step of the fog's walk is meters long; a sample next to a lamp made bright stripes); the range's fade and a spot's cone are taken at a jittered point of the stretch, the fog's density and what it lets through where the walk passes the point nearest the light.
- A pixel reads only a light's position and range first and skips the rest when it is out of reach or behind the surface.
- Cost on the GTX 980 at 1920 x 1080 (`forest_scene X Z TURN TILT lights`: 16 warm lamps in two rings 6 and 14 m around the camera, 800 frames): with a range of 12 m +1.3 to 1.7 ms in medium and +1.7 to 2.1 ms in high in the four measured views; with a range of 6 m +0.7 to 1.1 and +1.0 to 1.4 ms. The fog's share is 0.2 to 0.3 ms; the rest is surfaces genuinely in reach of many lights.
- Test: `tests/render/lights_render_test.cpp` (in the dark a sphere lit by a point light from its right shows 163 on that side and 0 on the other; a spot light from 3 m up lights the floor 10 degrees from its axis (244) and leaves it black at 25 degrees with an outer angle of 20; a box between it and the floor leaves a shadow (0 against 243 beside it, 247 at the same place without shadows); both backends). Example: `examples/render/lights` (three colored lamps and a flashlight with a shadow at night, in fog).
- Limits: point lights cast no shadows; spot light shadows come from meshes only, not terrain or impostors, and at most four; no light through thin leaves from local lights; the tiles have no depth range (a light far behind a near wall still visits that wall's tiles, then contributes nothing); a spot's beam right at the lamp is noisy in the fog without temporal anti-aliasing.

## Blended transparency

`Material::blend` takes glTF's three alpha modes (`BlendMode` in `renderer.h`): `Opaque` (alpha ignored; an `alpha_cutoff` above 0 still cuts, as before), `Cutout` (pixels under `alpha_cutoff` are not drawn, 0.5 when it is 0: leaves, fences), `Blend` (see-through by alpha: glass, a window, a bottle).

- How (`passes/blend_pass.cpp`, shader `mesh_blend.frag.hlsl` = `mesh.frag.hlsl` with `BLENDED`): blended draws (`DrawMesh`, `DrawSkinnedMesh`, `DrawModel`) are kept apart from the solid ones and drawn in a pass of their own after water and volumetric fog and before temporal anti-aliasing, over the single-sample scene image. Objects are sorted by the distance of the middle of their box from the camera, farthest first (a stable sort: equal distances keep the order of the calls), and mixed over the scene as `color * alpha + scene * (1 - alpha)`, where alpha is the material color's times the texture's. Each pixel is lit by `ShadeSurface` like any material (sun with shadows, cloud shadows, sky light, reflections, emission). The shader tests the depth pass's image itself and writes no depth (the pass also reads that image, so it cannot be its depth target); the depth pass runs whenever something is blended.
- Fog: the simple fog and the haze of distance at the surface's own distance (`ShadeSurface`). With volumetric fog, the fog in front of the surface is worked out from the fog walked to the scene behind it: the part let through to the surface is the part let through to the scene raised to the share of the fog's density (summed in 8 steps along the ray, with its height falloff) that lies before the surface, and the fog's light grows with the part it blocks. Mixed over the fogged scene by alpha, this gives exactly the same result as fogging the mix, so a pane keeps its tint in front of a fogged landscape.
- **Shadows: blended objects cast none** (chosen over treating them as cut out: clear glass throws almost no shadow, and a cut-out shadow of a window would be a solid black slab). They are left out of the shadow, depth and ambient occlusion passes. An object that needs a shadow (a stained curtain) can be drawn a second time with a `Cutout` material that only the shadow would see, but there is no shadow-only switch yet.
- Test: `tests/render/blend_render_test.cpp` (red glass at alpha 0.5 over a white wall gives 255 187 187 on screen, the expected half; a blue pane nearer than a red one, drawn first, is mixed after it: 187 137 225, the wrong order would give about 225 137 188; a pane behind the wall is hidden; in dense volumetric fog the glass still tints; both backends). Example: `examples/render/glass`.
- Limits: sorting is by object, not by triangle, so two objects that pass through each other, or the inside and outside of one double-sided object, can show in the wrong order; levels of detail of a blended object switch without the dithered fade; blended materials cannot be instanced (`DrawMeshInstances` refuses them); particles are drawn after temporal anti-aliasing and so over glass even when behind it; a moving pane has no motion vectors of its own (temporal anti-aliasing follows what is behind it, and the clipping of its history keeps the smear short); no refraction or frosted glass.

## Particles

Drawn by `Renderer::DrawParticles` (cards facing the camera, after TAA and before bloom, hidden and softened by the depth image), moved by `ParticleEmitter` in the world module: see [particles.md](particles.md).

## Decals

Prints, blood, bullet holes, puddles: pictures pressed onto the surfaces already drawn. The renderer draws forward (no G-buffer), so decals are projected from a box onto the depth image, in a pass of their own after the opaque scene (grass included, so blood lies on grass) and before water (`passes/decal_pass.cpp`, shaders in `shaders/decals/`, recording in `resources/decals.cpp`). Header: `poligon/render/decal.h`.

- `Renderer::DrawDecal(const glm::mat4& transform, const DecalMaterial& material)`: the box is the cube from -0.5 to 0.5 placed by `transform`; the picture lies in its x-z plane (x along the box's x, the picture's top edge along -z, as `CreatePlaneMesh`) and is pressed along its y axis. `DecalMaterial`: `texture` (color with alpha), optional `normal_texture`, `color`, `metallic`, `roughness`, `opacity` (fade a decal out over time), `edge_fade` (share of the half size at each face over which it fades out; 0.2), `max_angle_degrees` (60).
- How: the box's back faces are drawn without a depth test, so each pixel that might lie inside is visited once, also with the camera inside the box. The fragment shader rebuilds the world position from the depth image (`WorldPositionFromDepth` in `common.hlsli`, shared with water), moves it into the box, leaves it out when it is outside, fades it near the faces, finds the surface's normal from neighboring depths (the nearer neighbor on each axis, as ambient occlusion does) and leaves out surfaces turned more than `max_angle_degrees` from the box's y axis (a print does not climb a trunk). The point is then lit with `ShadeSurface` like any material (sun with shadows, cloud shadows, sky light, ambient occlusion, fog and haze) and blended over the scene with the picture's alpha. Decals are laid on in the order of the calls; consecutive calls with the same material are one instanced draw (each decal 128 bytes: the transform and its inverse). Boxes outside the view are skipped. With decals the depth pass always runs.
- Cost: about the pixels the boxes cover on screen; hundreds of small decals a frame are the intended load.
- Prints: `FootprintTrail` (`poligon/world/footprints.h`) lays a decal transform every `stride` meters a walker goes, left and right in turn, turned the way walked, keeps the last `max_prints` (50), starts afresh after a jump of more than 5 m, and asks a callback whether the ground there takes prints; `Draw(renderer, material)` draws them. It is game state (update it in `FixedUpdate`). Test: `tests/world/footprints_test.cpp`. `forest_scene` lays the player's prints where `GroundCover::GetDensity` gives fewer than 4 grass plants per square meter, starting with a trail walked in 40 m to the start, and one blood splash by the fox that stands still.
- Test: `tests/render/decal_render_test.cpp` (a red decal colors the floor in its box, leaves the floor outside and a wall standing in the box untouched, and is darker where a slab shades it: 224 lit, 97 in shadow). Example: `examples/render/decals`.
- Limits: a decal replaces the lighting of what it covers rather than adding to that surface's own material (a print on a rock is lit as the decal's roughness, not the rock's); decals land on everything opaque in the box that faces up enough, moving objects included (an animal walking through a puddle's box gets the puddle; no object can be excluded yet); the normal is worked out from depth, so it is coarse on grass and at outlines.

## Render targets (a second camera)

A scope, binoculars or a mirror: a picture drawn by a second camera and shown as a texture. The calls are on `Renderer`, outside `BeginFrame` and `EndFrame` (code in `renderer/render_targets.cpp`):

- `RenderTargetHandle CreateRenderTarget(int width, int height)` (16 to 8192 pixels each way), `ResizeRenderTarget`, `DestroyRenderTarget`, `TextureHandle GetRenderTargetTexture(RenderTargetHandle)`.
- A target's frame: `BeginFrame(camera, light, environment, target)`, draws as usual, `EndFrame()`. Then, in the window's frame, the texture goes into a `Material` (on a mesh: a mirror, a screen) or into `DrawSprite` (over the picture: a scope). Draw the target first; the window's frame then sees its latest picture.
- How: every view keeps its own images and histories (`ViewState` in `renderer_internal.h`: scene, depth, ambient occlusion, fog, bloom, exposure, cloud and anti-aliasing histories, motion of drawn objects). A target's `BeginFrame` swaps its view into the renderer's fields and `EndFrame` swaps it back (`Impl::SwapView`), so every pass runs for it unchanged: shadows from its own camera, fog, clouds, and temporal anti-aliasing that follows it frame by frame. The quality preset's render scale applies to it too.
- Cost: a frame of its own, roughly in proportion to its pixels plus the fixed cost of the passes (shadow cascades are drawn again). A target not drawn costs only its memory. So draw a scope at a fraction of the window's size and only in the frames it is at the eye; after a pause its histories catch up within a few frames.
- The sky light map is built over the window's frames only; a target uses the window's (it is the same sky). `GetFrameStats` after a target's `EndFrame` describes that frame.
- Test: `tests/render/render_target_test.cpp` (a 256 x 256 target sees a red cube on a green floor from a second camera; a plane in the window's view wearing it shows red in its middle and green at its edge; still right after resizing to 64 x 64; the window's frame counts only its own plane; both backends). Example: `examples/render/render_target` (binoculars: the view six times narrower, 180 x 180, in a disc in the corner).
- Limits: the window must be drawn by the plain `BeginFrame`; a target cannot be read in its own frame (no recursion: a mirror in a mirror shows the previous frame's picture at best, and only through a second target).

## 2D and text

HUD, crosshair, markers and subtitles are drawn over the finished picture: after tone mapping (so exposure, grading and bloom leave them alone) and before the debug windows, in a pass of their own (`passes/overlay_pass.cpp`, shaders in `shaders/overlay/`). Header: `poligon/render/overlay.h` (`Rect`, `FontHandle`); the calls are on `Renderer`, between `BeginFrame` and `EndFrame`:

- `DrawRect(const Rect& rect, Color color)`, `DrawSprite(TextureHandle texture, const Rect& rect, const Rect& uv, Color color)`, `DrawText(FontHandle font, const char* utf8, glm::vec2 position, float size, Color color)`, `glm::vec2 MeasureText(FontHandle font, const char* utf8, float size)`.
- Positions and sizes are pixels of the output image, (0, 0) at its top-left corner, y down; after `Resize` they simply refer to the new size. Colors are given as they look on screen (sRGB) with alpha; blending happens in linear light. Later calls are drawn over earlier ones; consecutive calls with the same texture share one draw call (all quads of a frame sit in one buffer, 48 bytes each).
- Text: `position` is the top-left corner of the first line, `size` the line height in pixels, a line break starts a new line. `GetBuiltinFont()` is always there and needs no file: printable ASCII from `stb_easy_font` (stb package, MIT or public domain), drawn into an atlas at startup, 12 pixels a line and sharp at whole multiples of that. `CreateTrueTypeFont(data, size, pixel_height)` makes a font from the contents of a TrueType file with `stb_truetype` (same package): printable ASCII, the Polish letters and the degree sign are packed into an atlas at `pixel_height` (2 x 2 oversampling); other sizes are scaled from it. It is not called `CreateFont` because `windows.h` defines that name as a macro. A character a font lacks is drawn as a hollow box.
- Test: `tests/render/overlay_render_test.cpp` (a rectangle covers exactly its pixels after `Resize`; 0.5 on screen is stored as 128 and half-transparent white over black as 188; text in the built-in font is as wide and high as `MeasureText` says; text over a scene changes only pixels inside its own box; a TrueType font, read from `C:/Windows/Fonts/arial.ttf` when the system has it and skipped otherwise, draws Polish letters and refuses data that is not a font). Example: `examples/render/hud`.
- Limits: no rotation or clipping rectangles, no kerning or text shaping (right-to-left, ligatures), one atlas size per TrueType font. `stb_truetype` does not check the bounds of what it reads, so font files must be ones the game ships (only the signature and table directory are checked).

## Shaders

- Plain HLSL, compiled at build time by DXC to DXIL (D3D12) and SPIR-V (Vulkan), then embedded in the engine library as byte arrays. There are no shader files to find at run time.
- A new shader: a file in the fitting group of `engine/src/render/shaders/` and an entry `poligon_embed_shader(...)` in `engine/src/render/CMakeLists.txt`. Files several shaders include go to `shaders/common` (on the include path of every shader).
- SDL GPU rules that HLSL must follow:
  - vertex inputs use the semantics `TEXCOORD0`, `TEXCOORD1`, ... (the number is the attribute location);
  - uniform buffers of a vertex shader: `register(bN, space1)`; of a fragment shader: `register(bN, space3)`;
  - textures and samplers of a vertex shader: `space0`; of a fragment shader: `space2`;
  - for Vulkan a texture and its sampler must be one combined binding: both get `[[vk::combinedImageSampler]][[vk::binding(N, 2)]]` with the same N (pattern in `mesh.frag.hlsl`). The DXIL compile skips these attributes.
- The C++ structs of uniforms (`VertexFrameUniforms`, `FragmentFrameUniforms`, `ObjectUniforms`, `MaterialUniforms` and the rest, in `engine/src/render/renderer/renderer_internal.h`) must match the `cbuffer`s of the same names in HLSL.

## Verifying images

- `--dump-frame FILE.png` saves the last frame when the run ends.
- `--gpu-validation` turns on the debug layer; tests with this flag fail on any `D3D12 ERROR` / `D3D12 WARNING` message.
- `image_compare A.png B.png [--channel-tolerance N] [--max-different-percent P]` compares two images with a tolerance (default: a difference of up to 2 per channel, up to 0.1% of pixels). Exit code 0 = match, 1 = no match.
- On one backend, images from two runs are identical, so they are compared with the tight default tolerance. Between Direct3D 12 and Vulkan we use `--channel-tolerance 12`: the drivers compute anisotropic texture filtering slightly differently (measured: up to 32 of 255 on a few hundred edge pixels). Without anisotropy both backends give bit-identical images.
- Mipmaps are computed by us on the CPU (`MakeHalfSizeImage`), so texture data is the same on every backend and driver.
- Reference image of the toy game: `tests/reference/toy_tick_45.png`. After an intended change of look: view the new dump from `build/<preset>/test_output/` and only then copy it over the reference.

## Known limits

- One light (the sun) plus sky light; no point or spot lights. No true transparency (only cut-out). No reflections of the scene other than the water's screen-space ones; everything else reflects only the sky light map.
- Ambient occlusion and fog see only what is on screen and only the nearest surface of each pixel. Fog is lit by the sun only within `shadow_distance` (beyond it, it counts as lit) and is walked at most 1000 m.
- Culling is per mesh and by view only: a mesh hidden behind another is still drawn.
- The scene's geometry is drawn one more time for the depth pass whenever ambient occlusion, volumetric fog or TAA is on, or multisampling is off; without multisampling that pass then saves more than it costs (see Graphics quality).
- Textures made from images (`CreateTexture(const Image&)`) are uncompressed (4 bytes per pixel plus mipmaps); cooked assets are block-compressed (a quarter to an eighth of that, see [assets.md](assets.md)).
- The alpha channel of a texture is used only for cut-out (`alpha_cutoff`), never for blending.
- Wind gusts and water waves repeat every 4096 m (their noise is anchored to the nearest whole multiple of 4096 m). A scene moved by a distance that is not a multiple of 4096 m has the same look but other gusts and waves; textures and clouds are fixed to the world, so a moved scene shows them at another place of their pattern.
- The terrain generator, plant rules and their maps still work in float map coordinates (6 x 6 km, about 0.5 mm at the far edge); they move to double with world streaming.
